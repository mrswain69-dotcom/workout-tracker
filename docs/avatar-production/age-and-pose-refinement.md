# Visible ageing and pose variety — 9 October 2026

User feedback after the Rugby release: the silhouettes repeat almost unchanged, and higher-tier characters still read as children despite improved clothes, awards and strength. Giving a prompt an adult age did not produce sufficient visual ageing. This supersedes the earlier gentle-ageing direction.

Keep one recognisable person through each ladder, while changing their facial proportions, stance and expression. Stable skin tone, eye shape and hair character preserve continuity; the rounded junior face, oversized eyes and head/body proportions must mature. Both male and female adult portraits need to read as adults without relying on muscles, beard, makeup or clothing. Mature does not require photorealism or exaggerated wrinkles.

## Stage progression

| Tier | Character age direction | Presentation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | 12–14 | Junior, relaxed introduction |
| Silver | 15–17 | Older teen, early recognition |
| Gold | 18–22 | Young adult, controlled sporting stance |
| Platinum | 23–27 | Clearly adult, assured movement |
| Diamond | 26–30 | Experienced adult, leadership |
| Elite | 28–33 | Mature athlete, restrained celebration |
| Champion | 30–36 | Seasoned champion, confident presentation |
| Unreal | 32–40 | Mature formal recognition |

These are fictional character ages, not a requirement for the user to age or change their body to earn an avatar. Session thresholds and rewards stay unchanged.

## Avoiding a sport stereotype

A character is one person's career story, not a representative face or body type for a sport. Preserve diversity across the overall catalogue. A later recurring-character roster could show a recognisable athlete enjoying several sports; that would require its own character/reference plan and does not justify generating extra collections now. Changing the person at every tier would weaken the current progression story, so retain identity while allowing credible growth.

## Review and targeted correction

1. Review Rugby first, then Football, comparing junior and adult faces and all eight silhouettes in each appearance. Existing assets remain live while this review is queued.
2. Produce a visibly mature Unreal pair only when a corrective image pass is started. Use existing Unreal as a suit/award/style reference, not an adult-anatomy anchor. Accept the pair as adult references only after visual inspection.
3. Check Gold through Champion against the adult anchor. Revise only portraits whose face or pose fails review. Bronze/Silver may remain when their age and stance fit the revised brief. This update records the review and new production direction; it does not regenerate any images.
4. Vary facing direction, feet, hand/equipment placement and expression. Use sport-specific controlled action, walking, leadership, celebration and formal poses. Keep readable equipment, trophy limits, transparent margins and equivalent prestige between appearances.
5. Preserve originals and generation provenance; record the specific failure and revision reason. Existing images remain functionally complete, but must not be described as passing the new age/pose gate until reviewed and corrected.

Cricket and later new batches follow the revised gate from their first images. Their adult anchor is checked before completing the remaining tiers. The offline prompt helper consumes both age stages and silhouette direction from the canonical JSON.
